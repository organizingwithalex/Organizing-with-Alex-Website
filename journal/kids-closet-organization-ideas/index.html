How to Organize a Kids' Closet That Grows With Them

A kids' closet should work for your child now and in two years. Here's how we set them up.

Kids grow fast, and so does their stuff. A well-planned closet makes getting dressed easier, gives kids some independence, and saves you from digging through bins of outgrown clothes. Here's how we set up kids' closets for families across Palm Beach County.

Edit by size first

Take everything out and sort it into three groups: fits now, too big, and outgrown. Outgrown clothes can be donated, passed down or stored. Too-big clothes go into labeled bins by size, so they're ready when your child needs them.

Organize by height

  • Low: what your child uses every day. Shoes, pajamas, everyday outfits and a basket for favorite things.
  • Middle: books, accessories and items you help with, like hair bows or special shoes.
  • High: next-size-up bins, keepsakes and seasonal items.

If the closet has two hanging rods, use the lower one for your child's everyday clothes so they can reach them on their own.

Use matching hangers and dividers

Slim, matching kids' hangers make a small closet look calm and fit more. Group by type (dresses, tops, bottoms, jackets) and then by color. Shelf dividers keep folded stacks neat.

Make shoes easy

Use a shoe shelf or tiered rack at child height. Pair shoes on open shelves so kids can find and put them away without help.

Give books and toys a home

Many kids' closets work beautifully with a few shelves of books sorted by color or size, and soft baskets for stuffed animals. Rotate toys in and out instead of keeping everything at once.

Clear bins for small things

Hair accessories, socks and small toys disappear into big baskets. Small clear drawers or bins with labels keep them visible and easy to put back.

Revisit every season

A quick reset each season, when sizes change or the weather shifts, keeps the closet working. Our Seasonal Reset plan is designed for exactly that.
